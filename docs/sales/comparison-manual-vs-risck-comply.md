# Spreadsheet / Manual Process vs RISCK COMPLY

## Executive summary

Spreadsheets are useful to start a compliance process, but they become fragile when the company needs ownership, review cycles, vendor evidence, audit history and executive visibility.

RISCK COMPLY is designed to turn scattered compliance work into an accountable operating system.

## Comparison table

| Area | Spreadsheet / manual process | RISCK COMPLY |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk ownership | Owner often stored as plain text and becomes outdated. | Owners are part of the workflow and can be reviewed with status and actions. |
| Document control | Files are spread across folders and versions. | Documents can be tracked with owner, status, review date and evidence context. |
| Vendor control | Vendor evidence is often hidden in email or procurement folders. | Vendor register centralizes criticality, DPA status and evidence gaps. |
| Deadlines | Manual reminders and calendar events. | Deadline visibility can be connected to compliance actions and owners. |
| Audit readiness | Evidence is collected manually when requested. | Evidence can be organized continuously and reviewed before external pressure. |
| Executive reporting | Manual slide creation and spreadsheet summaries. | Dashboard view of open actions, risk posture and missing evidence. |
| Accountability | Hard to know who changed what and why. | Activity history supports review of important changes. |
| Scalability | Breaks as teams, countries, vendors and documents grow. | Designed for structured growth across teams and workflows. |
| Buyer confidence | Answers depend on manual follow-up. | Answers can be tied to structured records and evidence. |

## When spreadsheets are still acceptable

A spreadsheet may be enough when:

- The company has very few vendors.
- One person owns all compliance work.
- There are no enterprise customer reviews.
- There is no need for audit history.
- Review dates and owners rarely change.

## When RISCK COMPLY becomes valuable

RISCK COMPLY becomes valuable when:

- Several people own compliance tasks.
- Vendors and DPAs need review.
- Customers ask for proof.
- Policies need review cycles.
- Leadership wants visibility.
- Audit preparation is taking too much time.
