# RISCK COMPLY — Buyer M&A Q&A

Date: 2026-10-07

**Why sell now?**  
The product was completed before full commercial scaling, and the ownership strategy shifted toward a strategic transfer to an organization with greater distribution and integration capacity.

**Why not launch and scale it independently?**  
The current strategy prioritizes a strategic sale rather than building the commercial organization required for long-term standalone scaling.

**Why is there no revenue?**  
RISCK COMPLY is pre-commercial. Current verified product billing evidence shows no established revenue base. The acquisition case is therefore technology/IP and build-vs-buy value, not ARR.

**How many customers are there?**  
No established customer base is claimed. Product use cases are presented as use cases, not customer case studies.

**Why buy instead of build?**  
A buyer can evaluate an already-built AI-governance product, regulatory-domain work, multi-tenant enterprise architecture, security/control design, evidence/documentation flows and integration optionality instead of starting from zero.

**Who owns the IP?**  
Repository/product control is evidenced, but the current M&A title pack does not credit the creator-to-seller title transfer as executed. That chain is an explicit legal/closing item and must not be described as clean until signed evidence exists.

**What is included in a sale?**  
Potential scope includes source code, repository, software IP, documentation, schema, brand/domain, design/marketing assets, compliance content, deployment configuration, provider handoff and knowledge transfer, subject to title and provider-specific transfer confirmation.

**What is not automatically included?**  
Personal accounts, unrelated seller assets, credentials/secrets, third-party rights not owned by the seller, and provider accounts that cannot legally/operationally be transferred as-is.

**What security controls exist?**  
Documented controls include RBAC, RLS tenant isolation, BOLA/IDOR-oriented authorization controls, fail-closed data-access patterns, audit/evidence controls, CI security gates, SBOM/OSS diligence and extensive security runbooks.

**What is the pentest status?**  
Internal/external security evidence exists, but a clean independent terminal pentest/retest is not currently credited as PASS in the canonical M&A control tower.

**What certifications exist?**  
No SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification is claimed unless independently obtained and evidenced.

**Is it enterprise-ready?**  
The architecture and control set are enterprise-oriented and buyer diligence materials are internally ready. Current external/transaction gaps — including production SHA parity, terminal independent assurance, title execution and provider closing steps — remain disclosed.

**What remains external?**  
Buyer decisions, NDA/LOI economics, formal diligence acceptance, legal/tax/accounting evidence, executed title instruments, provider transfer actions, signatures, funds flow and closing acceptance.

**How will provider transfer work?**  
Provider by provider: project/account ownership transfer where supported, buyer-created replacement/migration where required, billing/legal-identity reconciliation, secret rotation and post-transfer validation. Blanket account-transfer claims are avoided.

**How will code transfer work?**  
Normally through a buyer-controlled GitHub organization/account or agreed source-delivery method after transaction controls are in place. Source code is not auto-shared before explicit approval.

**How will domain transfer work?**  
Through registrar-controlled transfer/account-change mechanics after registrar title/control evidence and transaction authorization are confirmed.

**How will knowledge transfer work?**  
Through structured architecture, deployment, security, provider, AI-governance and operational handoff led by the primary creator, with scope and duration negotiated in the transaction.

**How quickly could a buyer deploy or rebrand it?**  
The product is built, but no universal timeline is promised. Timing depends on buyer architecture, security review, provider migration, branding, production remediation and transaction close.

**Why is RISCK COMPLY relevant to AI governance?**  
It centralizes AI inventory, classification, ownership, assessment, evidence, documentation, monitoring and enterprise governance workflows.

**Why is it relevant to the EU AI Act?**  
The product contains workflows and documentation aligned to AI Act governance areas, while explicitly avoiding any claim that software use itself constitutes legal approval or compliance certification.
