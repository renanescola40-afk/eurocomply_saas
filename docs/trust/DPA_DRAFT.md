## Breach-notice position

The canonical DPA structure uses the GDPR Article 33(2) processor standard of notification to the customer/controller **without undue delay after awareness**, with reasonably available incident information.

Do not invent a tighter fixed contractual number of hours unless the signed agreement and operational capability support it.

## SCC / transfer boundary

Article 28 contracting and GDPR Chapter V transfers are separate controls.

Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/915 controller-processor clauses support Article 28 contracting. They do not by themselves close Chapter V for a third-country transfer. Flow-level SCC/adequacy/DPF/TIA conclusions remain provider- and fact-specific.

## Current finalisation blockers

The DPA must not be represented as an effective signed contract until the applicable final facts are attributable, including:

- contracting legal entity and required registered details;
- customer/order-specific scope where needed;
- final active subprocessors and their relevant purposes/locations;
- transfer mechanism for actual in-scope third-country flows;
- final retention/deletion and provider lifecycle facts;
- contract-grade TOM schedule;
- final incorporation/signature path;
- commercial clauses requiring final agreement; and
- qualified review when law, contract or buyer requirement actually requires it.

Internal documentation completeness is not a substitute for those facts.
