# RISCK COMPLY — Addressable Market Claims Substantiation

Date: 2026-10-06  
Purpose: M&A requirement 90 — transaction-grade substantiation of addressable-market claims  
Status: CLOSED_FOR_DILIGENCE_SUBSTANTIATION

## Executive conclusion

RISCK COMPLY operates in a demonstrably broad regulatory market, but this document deliberately avoids equating the entire EU business population with a serviceable or obtainable market.

The evidence supports the following bounded claims:

1. The EU business economy contained approximately **34.0 million enterprises in 2024** according to Eurostat.
2. The EU AI Act applies across multiple operator roles, including providers, deployers, importers and distributors, and can also apply to providers/deployers established outside the EU where AI-system output is used in the Union.
3. Therefore, the regulatory addressable universe is structurally cross-sector and cross-border.
4. RISCK COMPLY's practical serviceable market must be filtered to organizations that actually develop, provide, deploy, import, distribute or otherwise govern AI systems within the AI Act scope and that have a willingness or need to procure external compliance tooling.
5. No claim is made here that all EU enterprises use AI, fall within a material AI Act duty, need RISCK COMPLY, or are commercially reachable.

## Primary-source market anchors

### EU enterprise base

Eurostat reported approximately **34.0 million enterprises** active in the EU business economy in 2024, employing about 164.5 million people.

Source:
- Eurostat, "EU businesses: €10.9 trillion in value added in 2024", published 2026-09-30.
- Eurostat Structural Business Statistics overview, data extracted September 2026.

### Regulatory scope

Article 2 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applies, among others, to:
- providers placing AI systems or general-purpose AI models on the Union market;
- deployers established or located in the Union;
- certain providers and deployers established outside the Union where AI-system output is used in the Union;
- importers and distributors;
- certain product manufacturers and authorised representatives.

This makes the relevant compliance market broader than EU-headquartered software vendors alone.

Source:
- EUR-Lex consolidated Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Article 2, consolidated version current to 2026-07-27.

### Active compliance demand signal

The European Commission published Article 50 transparency guidance in July 2026 and states that the relevant transparency obligations apply from 2026-08-02. This is evidence of an active compliance implementation cycle rather than a purely future regulatory thesis.

Source:
- European Commission, Guidelines on transparency obligations for providers and deployers of AI systems, 2026-07-20.

## Market model permitted for buyer materials

### TAM framing

Permitted:
- "The EU contains approximately 34 million enterprises, while the AI Act creates obligations across providers, deployers and other AI value-chain actors in and, in defined cases, outside the EU."

Not permitted:
- "RISCK COMPLY has a 34 million customer TAM."
- "All EU companies need RISCK COMPLY."
- "34 million companies are subject to material AI Act compliance obligations."

### SAM framing

The serviceable available market should be defined using explicit filters, for example:
- organization uses, deploys, provides, imports, distributes or governs AI;
- relevant operations or AI outputs fall within EU AI Act territorial scope;
- compliance duties are material enough to justify software/process spend;
- organization falls within the commercial ICP and supported geographies/languages;
- buying motion is compatible with RISCK COMPLY's product and procurement model.

Until a transaction-specific dataset is approved, SAM should be presented qualitatively or as scenario ranges, not as a single unsupported point estimate.

### SOM framing

Serviceable obtainable market must be based on actual commercial capacity and evidence such as:
- verified pipeline;
- observed response/conversion rates;
- pricing;
- sales-cycle duration;
- implementation/support capacity;
- partner/channel contribution.

No unsupported SOM percentage is approved.

## Buyer-safe claim set

APPROVED:
- "RISCK COMPLY addresses a large, cross-sector regulatory market created by the EU AI Act."
- "The EU business economy contains approximately 34 million enterprises, but RISCK COMPLY does not treat all of them as customers or regulated AI operators."
- "The AI Act has extraterritorial reach in defined circumstances, so the addressable compliance universe can include non-EU organizations whose AI-system outputs are used in the Union."
- "Market sizing must filter the enterprise population by AI use/operator role, regulatory relevance, ICP fit and procurement propensity."

NOT APPROVED:
- unsupported market-revenue forecasts;
- unsupported market-share percentages;
- treating all EU enterprises as AI Act buyers;
- treating regulatory scope as proof of purchase intent;
- paid-market-report numbers that are not retained as transaction evidence.

## Diligence closure rationale

Requirement 90 is considered CLOSED because:
- primary official sources establish the size of the EU enterprise base;
- primary law establishes the relevant operator and territorial scope;
- the claim hierarchy and exclusions prevent the common overstatement that the whole enterprise base is a commercial TAM;
- a reproducible TAM/SAM/SOM methodology is documented for buyer-facing use.

This closes substantiation of addressable-market claims. It does not represent revenue, market share, customer demand, or a valuation opinion.
