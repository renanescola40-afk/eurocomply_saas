## Exact remaining blockers

### 1. Entity and tax facts

Why: The public legal pages do not yet contain authoritative registered name, company identifier, registered office, signatory authority, VAT status/regime and confirmed activity facts.

Mandatory: Yes where the fact is required for company disclosure, contracting, invoicing or the applicable tax treatment. The exact company-specific VAT conclusion is a tax/accounting fact, not something to infer from Stripe settings.

Who requires it: Portuguese company/tax rules, contracting counterparties and procurement reviewers as applicable.

Evidence that closes it: Current official Portuguese registry/company evidence; AT/VIES or attributable accountant/company evidence for tax facts; Stripe account reconciliation.

Free closure path: Owner retrieves the current official records and downloads the evidence. No paid lawyer is intrinsically required.

Owner action: Obtain the current registry extract and tax evidence, then approve the exact public identity/tax wording. Do not publish the correspondence address as the registered office.

### 2. Controller privacy notice and accountability

Why: Privacy is a review draft and still has unresolved controller identity, legal bases, recipients, retention/deletion and accountability mapping.

Mandatory: Yes for applicable GDPR processing and Articles 13/14 transparency. A lawyer is not automatically required; the controller remains accountable.

Who requires it: GDPR controller obligations and, where relevant, CNPD/EDPB enforcement expectations.

Evidence that closes it: Frozen data-flow/role matrix, approved legal-basis and retention decisions, final Articles 13/14 notice, DSR/breach process evidence and consistent public publication.

Free closure path: Internal controller decision plus technical/document consistency tests. Escalate only if a fact-specific DPIA/DPO/representative or buyer requirement actually triggers it.

Owner action: Confirm controller/processor roles, lawful bases, retention/deletion periods and privacy contact after the entity facts are obtained.

### 3. Article 28 DPA

Why: The DPA is a review draft, not an effective processor contract for customer data.

Mandatory: Yes when RISCK COMPLY processes customer personal data as processor. Not a launch blocker for a no-personal-data trial, but it is a blocker for processor activation.

Who requires it: GDPR Article 28 and the customer contract.

Evidence that closes it: Final Article 28 terms, current processing schedule, subprocessor authorization, transfer terms and a binding customer acceptance/signature path.

Free closure path: Use a self-authored Article 28 addendum and customer acceptance; use provider DPAs as upstream evidence.

Owner action: Approve the final DPA business/legal choices and bind it to the accepted Terms/order flow.

### 4. Provider, subprocessor and international-transfer facts

Why: General provider documentation is not account-specific proof. Region, role, retention, subprocessors and Chapter V mechanism remain unresolved for Vercel, Supabase, Stripe, Google, GitHub, Sentry, PostHog, Upstash and Resend.

Mandatory: Yes when those providers process personal data or enable third-country access. A transfer may be N/A for a particular flow only after the actual flow is verified.

Who requires it: GDPR controller/processor obligations, Article 28 schedules and customer procurement.

Evidence that closes it: Provider account settings/contracts, current DPA/subprocessor lists, region/retention exports, SCC/DPF or other valid mechanism where applicable, and a reconciled production provider register.

Free closure path: Provider dashboards, official trust/DPA/subprocessor pages and free support channels/forms. No email may be sent under current authorization.

Owner action: Authorize/perform any provider contract acceptance that is legally binding and confirm account-specific facts; do not treat unsigned PandaDoc/provider correspondence as executed.

### 5. ePrivacy, analytics and commercial communications

Why: Consent-gated design exists, but exact production storage, PostHog project, retention and send-time/suppression evidence are not frozen.

Mandatory: Purpose-dependent. Non-essential analytics/storage and direct marketing require the applicable consent/opt-out basis; if disabled, the corresponding processing path is not active.

Who requires it: Applicable Portuguese/ePrivacy rules and GDPR.

Evidence that closes it: Production browser/network test, consent-withdrawal test, cookie/storage inventory, provider facts and marketing suppression evidence—or a documented decision to keep non-essential paths disabled.

Free closure path: Disable non-essential analytics/marketing until verified, then test the actual production release.

Owner action: Decide whether optional analytics/marketing is enabled in the accepted release and approve the resulting notice.

### 6. Commercial Terms and binding publication

Why: Terms remain a review draft and are not effective. Entity identity, order/billing process, tax wording, refund/cancellation position, liability, governing law, notices and service boundary are not frozen.

Mandatory: Yes for a truthful commercial contract/publication position; a master legal opinion is not automatically required.

Who requires it: Applicable B2B/electronic-contracting rules and the parties' contract/procurement terms.

Evidence that closes it: Final entity facts, approved commercial decisions, effective Terms, incorporation into checkout/order form and consistency tests across pricing, billing and legal pages.

Free closure path: Internal commercial decisions and self-authored terms; use customer counsel only if the customer makes it a requirement.

Owner action: Approve the final commercial policy and the exact effective-order mechanism.

### 7. Current release and operational evidence

Why: Protected main is ahead of canonical Production, and current production telemetry still has unresolved reliability/security evidence. This is not itself proof of an AI Act or GDPR statutory violation, but it prevents a truthful final publication/release assertion.

Mandatory: Release/assurance gate for the claimed accepted release; not a standalone lawyer requirement.

Who requires it: RISCK internal release governance and any buyer that conditions acceptance on clean evidence.

Evidence that closes it: Promote the reviewed main commit only after checks pass, run the legal/publication regression suite and obtain a clean or explained operational/security retest.

Free closure path: CI, deployment and internal testing; no paid assessment is intrinsically required for legal launch.

Owner action: Approve production promotion and any external assessment engagement if selected.
