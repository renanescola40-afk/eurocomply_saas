## 1. Mission

Create qualified B2B conversations with European organisations that have a visible AI-governance operating problem.

This is not a bulk cold-email system.

Operating model:

`PUBLIC SIGNAL -> ACCOUNT HYPOTHESIS -> RELEVANT CONTENT -> HUMAN OUTREACH -> DISCOVERY -> QUALIFICATION -> PIPELINE`

Do not scrape personal emails, buy opaque lead lists, fabricate trigger events or imply surveillance.

---

## 2. Highest-fit initial account archetype

### Archetype A — European B2B AI provider

Strong fit when the company:

- develops/sells AI systems, assistants, agents or AI SaaS;
- sells to enterprise or regulated customers;
- has 10–500+ employees or meaningful enterprise deployment complexity;
- publicly talks about security, data sovereignty, GDPR, AI Act, human oversight, traceability or enterprise readiness;
- operates across multiple use cases/customers/jurisdictions;
- faces customer procurement questionnaires.

Why this archetype matters:

AI providers may need to manage their own systems/use cases, provider/deployer role context, customer evidence, review history, vendor/model dependencies and transparency obligations.

Do not assume they are legally in scope for a specific obligation without facts.

### Archetype B — AI-intensive European B2B deployer

Examples:

- HR/recruiting technology using AI;
- fintech/insurance AI workflows;
- customer-service AI;
- legal AI;
- industrial AI;
- healthcare-adjacent AI;
- logistics/supply-chain AI.

High fit when multiple departments, systems, vendors and customer reviews create governance complexity.

### Archetype C — Consultancy / implementation partner

Potential channel/partner rather than standard prospect when the organisation:

- helps clients implement AI;
- provides AI transformation or compliance advisory;
- serves multiple PME/ETI/mid-market accounts;
- needs a structured operating workspace for client governance projects.

## 3. Exclusion / caution list

Do not treat every company in AI governance as a prospect.

Separate:

- **PROSPECT** — potential RISCK COMPLY user/customer;
- **PARTNER** — consultancy/association/ecosystem route;
- **COMPETITOR** — sells overlapping AI governance software;
- **WATCH** — adjacent company where fit is unclear.

Avoid outbound to direct competitors using a disguised sales approach.

## 4. Account scoring — 100 points

### A. Governance relevance — 0–30

+30: enterprise AI product/workflow with explicit governance/security/compliance requirements
+22: enterprise AI product with data/security positioning
+15: AI-heavy SaaS but governance need not explicit
+5: generic software company with limited visible AI usage

### B. Public trigger strength — 0–25

+25: new enterprise AI launch, regulated/enterprise expansion, procurement/governance announcement, AI Act/security positioning
+18: recent AI product launch or major enterprise deployment
+10: ongoing AI offering without recent trigger
+0: no reliable public signal

### C. Operating complexity — 0–20

+20: multi-country / many enterprise customers / multiple AI products or agents
+15: enterprise product with several workflows/integrations
+8: small focused AI product

### D. Commercial accessibility — 0–15

+15: clear enterprise/demo/contact motion and active public thought leadership
+10: company reachable but buying team unclear
+5: very large enterprise with complex procurement

### E. Timing / market learning value — 0–10

+10: matches current EN/DE/FR/ES campaign wedge and can teach us something valuable
+5: good fit but less relevant to current campaign

### Bands

- 80–100: Tier A — personalized human outreach
- 65–79: Tier B — engage/content-first, outreach after signal
- 50–64: Tier C — monitor/nurture
- <50: do not spend manual sales time yet

Scores are internal hypotheses, not statements about buying intent.

## 5. Buying-group map

Never depend on one contact.

Potential visible/internal stakeholders:

- Founder / CEO for smaller AI vendors
- CTO / Head of AI
- Compliance / AI Governance
- Legal / DPO / Privacy
- Security / CISO / GRC
- Product / Engineering
- Procurement / Vendor Risk
- Enterprise Sales / Customer Trust

For early-stage AI vendors, the founder/CTO may own several of these responsibilities.

For larger companies, map at least three functions before calling an opportunity enterprise-ready.

## 6. Public-signal triggers

Valid triggers include:

- launch of AI agent/copilot/platform;
- expansion from pilot to enterprise deployment;
- new enterprise/regulated-market positioning;
- public claim about AI Act/GDPR/security/governance;
- new AI product line;
- marketplace/enterprise procurement expansion;
- AI-related funding or scale-up where governance complexity plausibly increases;
- publication about human oversight, evaluation, traceability or data sovereignty.

Invalid triggers:

- guessed private project;
- personal data inference;
- fabricated customer pressure;
- `I saw you visited our site` unless the person explicitly submitted/consented and the workflow supports that use.

## 7. Outreach philosophy

Outbound should be useful even if the prospect never buys.

Bad:

> We are the leading EU AI Act compliance platform and can make you compliant. Can I get 15 minutes?

Good:

> I saw you are moving AI agents into enterprise workflows. One pattern we keep seeing is that customer security/compliance reviews create a second operating problem: keeping system/use-case ownership, provider role context, evidence and review history connected. We built RISCK COMPLY around that workflow. I can send the AI inventory/evidence model we use if it is useful.

No fake personalization.

## 8. Five-touch controlled sequence — 21 days

### Touch 1 — useful observation

Channel: LinkedIn or legitimate business email where available through normal business channels.

Structure:

1. one real public signal;
2. one governance implication framed as hypothesis;
3. one useful asset/offering;
4. no pressure.

Example:

> Your move from AI pilots to enterprise deployment caught my attention. I’m working on the operational layer behind that transition — inventory, ownership, risk/evidence and review history. We just built a practical AI-system inventory model for European teams. Happy to send it if useful.

### Touch 2 — relevant insight, 3–5 days

Send one piece based on their problem:

- Article 50 decision map;
- provider/deployer operating model;
- AI inventory template;
- evidence checklist.

No `just following up` message without value.

### Touch 3 — one diagnostic question, 7–10 days

Example:

> When an enterprise customer asks for your AI governance process today, is the answer assembled from product/security/legal separately, or do you already have one system-of-record for systems, owners and evidence?

### Touch 4 — proof / trust angle, 14 days

If relevant:

> One reason we built a public Trust Center is that enterprise procurement often becomes the real bottleneck after product fit. If buyer evidence is part of what you’re solving, I can share the structure we use.

Do not claim certifications not held.

### Touch 5 — close loop, 21 days

> I’ll close the loop here rather than keep filling your inbox. If AI inventory/evidence becomes a priority later, I’m happy to compare operating models.

Stop unless the account re-engages or a genuinely new trigger appears.

## 11. Pipeline rule

An account becomes an opportunity only when there is a real two-way commercial signal such as:

- discovery/demo accepted;
- concrete governance problem confirmed;
- buying process/timing discussed;
- procurement/security review begins;
- self-service commercial action occurs.

A LinkedIn connection or reply is not automatically an opportunity.
