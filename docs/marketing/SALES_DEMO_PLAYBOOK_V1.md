## 1. Mission

Turn qualified interest into a useful buyer conversation without overclaiming legal outcomes, certifications, production readiness or ROI.

Commercial principle:

`DISCOVER THE OPERATING PROBLEM -> MAP THE CURRENT WORKFLOW -> SHOW ONLY RELEVANT PRODUCT PROOF -> SURFACE TRUST/PROCUREMENT NEEDS -> AGREE A NEXT STEP`

A RISCK COMPLY demo is not a feature tour.

It is an operational AI-governance review.

## 3. 30-minute demo structure

### Minute 0–3 — Trigger

Ask:

1. What happened that made AI governance a priority now?
2. Is this being driven by customer procurement, board oversight, internal policy, AI rollout, the EU AI Act, security review or something else?
3. What would happen if the organization did nothing for the next six months?

Do not open with a product pitch.

### Minute 3–8 — Current operating model

Ask:

- Where is the AI inventory today?
- Who owns it?
- How many systems or use cases are in scope?
- Which teams add or review records?
- How are providers/vendors tracked?
- Where are risk assessments stored?
- Where are policies and supporting evidence stored?
- How do you know when something changes or a review becomes stale?
- How do you answer customer/procurement questions today?

Listen for:

`SPREADSHEET -> EMAIL -> DRIVE/NOTION -> MANUAL CHASING -> NO SINGLE OWNER -> NO REVIEW HISTORY`

### Minute 8–10 — Confirm the problem

Use a short synthesis:

> What I’m hearing is that the information exists, but ownership, review state and evidence are spread across several places. The problem is less about creating another policy and more about operating the process consistently. Is that accurate?

Do not continue until buyer confirms or corrects the summary.

### Minute 10–22 — Relevant product proof

Show only the workflow corresponding to the primary pain.

#### If AI Inventory is primary

Show:

1. system/use-case record;
2. owner;
3. provider;
4. country/context;
5. risk/review state;
6. evidence gaps;
7. history / next review.

Story:

`KNOW WHAT EXISTS -> KNOW WHO OWNS IT -> KNOW WHAT NEEDS REVIEW`

#### If EU AI Act readiness is primary

Show:

1. inventory;
2. organization/system role context;
3. assessment workflow;
4. documented rationale;
5. evidence and actions;
6. review lifecycle.

Never present the product as an automatic legal-classification oracle.

#### If AI vendor risk / procurement is primary

Show:

1. provider/vendor record;
2. linked systems/use cases;
3. evidence requests;
4. security/privacy/governance context;
5. findings/actions;
6. review/renewal state.

#### If evidence/audit/customer review is primary

Show:

1. linked records;
2. policies/documents;
3. evidence packs;
4. decisions/owners;
5. activity history;
6. Trust Center.

#### If multi-team governance is primary

Show:

1. assignments;
2. tasks;
3. review states;
4. approval/ownership;
5. organization boundaries;
6. Enterprise path where supported by current product truth.

### Minute 22–26 — Trust and procurement

Ask:

- Will security review be required?
- Will a DPA be required?
- Does procurement require a vendor questionnaire?
- Will SSO/SCIM/RBAC requirements matter?
- Do you need a specific hosting/data-region answer?
- Who signs off internally?

Show Trust Center only at the level supported by current evidence.

Never claim certifications or completed independent pentest unless current accepted evidence exists.

### Minute 26–30 — Commercial next step

Choose one next step only.

Possible next steps:

- self-service plan evaluation;
- Business assisted rollout discussion;
- Enterprise procurement review;
- security/DPA review;
- scoped second demo with another stakeholder;
- AI Inventory Template / readiness guide for a not-yet-ready account.

Do not end with vague `we’ll follow up` language.

## 4. Discovery question bank

### Inventory

- How many AI tools, systems or use cases do you believe exist today?
- How confident are you that the list is complete?
- Who can add a new system?
- What happens when a team starts using a new external AI tool?
- Do you distinguish a vendor from the internal use case built around it?

### Ownership

- Is there an accountable owner for every system?
- Who owns risk review?
- Who owns vendor assurance?
- Who approves production use?
- What happens when an owner leaves?

### Risk

- How is risk context documented today?
- Can someone understand the rationale six months later?
- What triggers reassessment?
- Are changes to provider, data or use case linked to review?

### Evidence

- If a customer asked for governance evidence today, where would the team collect it from?
- How long does that process take?
- Do you know which evidence is current versus stale?
- Can evidence be traced back to a system, decision, control or owner?

### Procurement

- Does procurement review AI vendors separately from other SaaS vendors?
- Which documents or answers cause the most delay?
- Does the same vendor support multiple AI use cases?
- How are renewals and material changes reviewed?

### Regulation

- Which EU AI Act obligations are actually driving work now?
- Is the challenge interpreting the law, or operationalizing decisions after interpretation?
- Who provides legal interpretation internally or externally?

### Commercial

- What budget or operating process already exists around governance/GRC/security/privacy?
- Is there an approved initiative or are you still building the case?
- Who needs to approve software spend?
- What would make this project fail internally?

## 6. Objection handling

### “We can do this in Excel.”

Response frame:

Agree with the valid part:

> For a small number of systems and one owner, a spreadsheet can absolutely work.

Then identify the breakpoint:

> The question is what happens when ownership changes, vendors multiply, reviews expire, evidence needs to be handed to customers, or several teams edit the process. RISCK COMPLY is designed for the point where the inventory becomes an operating workflow rather than a static list.

Do not attack spreadsheets as inherently bad.

### “We already have a GRC platform.”

> That may be the right system for enterprise controls. The question is whether your AI-specific inventory, use-case context, provider relationships, assessment rationale and evidence workflow already operate cleanly inside it. RISCK COMPLY should only be considered if there is an AI-governance operating gap worth solving.

Never claim the product replaces every GRC suite.

### “Can you guarantee EU AI Act compliance?”

> No. RISCK COMPLY supports AI-governance operations and readiness evidence. Legal obligations depend on the system, the organization’s role, use case and applicable law. Qualified legal interpretation remains necessary where appropriate.

### “Does the software automatically determine whether something is high-risk?”

> The platform can structure the facts, risk signals, review workflow and documented rationale. It should not be sold as a substitute for qualified legal classification.

### “Why not OneTrust / Credo / Saidot / another established vendor?”

> Those products may be appropriate depending on your organization. RISCK COMPLY’s focus is operational AI governance for European teams: making inventory, ownership, risk context, evidence and workflow easier to run and review. The right choice depends on your existing stack, complexity, procurement requirements and the depth of governance you need.

Never invent competitor pricing or weaknesses.

### “You are a new company.”

> That is a fair diligence question. We should earn trust through transparent product evidence, clear security boundaries, current Trust Center material and a procurement process that distinguishes what is implemented from what is still being completed. We do not substitute maturity claims for evidence.

### “Are you certified?”

Use current evidence only.

If no accepted certification exists:

> We do not claim that certification today. Our Trust Center separates implemented controls, provider-backed capabilities and items that still require independent or contractual evidence.

### “Have you completed an independent pentest?”

Use current Enterprise Control Tower state. Until accepted evidence exists:

> An independent pentest/retest remains a release-readiness item, so we do not claim a completed accepted pentest today.

### “Can we see your DPA/security pack?”

> We can use the current Trust Center and procurement materials as the starting point. Final legal/contractual material remains subject to the publication and legal-review gates applicable at the time of contracting.

## 7. Commercial guardrails

Never say:

- guaranteed compliance;
- audit guaranteed;
- officially EU approved;
- automatically legally classifies every system;
- certified when not certified;
- independently pentested when not accepted;
- 99.9% uptime unless contract/evidence supports it;
- “used by X customers” without verified truth;
- “saves X%” without measured customer evidence;
- “best / #1 / leading” without substantiation.

Prefer:

- helps;
- supports;
- designed to support;
- operational readiness;
- review-ready evidence;
- structured workflow;
- evidence-bound;
- based on current implementation.

## 8. Follow-up email structure

Do not send generic `thanks for your time` emails.

Use five blocks:

1. Buyer trigger
2. Current operating gap
3. Relevant RISCK COMPLY workflow
4. Proof/material requested
5. One agreed next step

Example structure:

Subject: AI governance next step — [company]

- Trigger: customer procurement review across several AI use cases
- Current gap: inventory and evidence spread between spreadsheet + Drive
- Relevant workflow: AI Inventory + ownership + evidence/review state
- Material: Trust Center + Inventory Template
- Next step: 30-minute review with Security/Procurement

Do not include unsupported commitments.
