# RISCK COMPLY — Existing LinkedIn Page Audit — 2 September 2026

Status: public-page audit. No LinkedIn mutation has been executed.

## Executive finding

The public LinkedIn company page is already live and contains useful early-stage educational content, but the existing feed should be normalized before autonomous publishing begins.

The main risks are not visual. They are claim precision, regulatory scope and brand consistency.

## Keep

- AI inventory as the recommended first operational step.
- Governance as an evidence/ownership problem rather than only a legal-document problem.
- Educational focus on EU AI Act readiness.
- B2B audience orientation.
- Website CTA to risckcomply.com.

## Correct or retire when LinkedIn write access is available

### 1. Brand spelling

Observed public copy uses variants including `RiskComply`, while the canonical brand is `RISCK COMPLY` / `Risck Comply`.

Action:
- standardize all future posts to `RISCK COMPLY`;
- update About/tagline where permissions allow;
- avoid creating a second brand identity through `RiskComply`.

### 2. Over-broad high-risk classifications

Some existing posts speak as if broad logistics, warehouse or supply-chain AI categories are automatically high-risk.

Risk:
- the AI Act high-risk determination is use-case and legal-context specific;
- broad sector wording can mislead buyers and weaken credibility.

Action:
- replace categorical sector claims with use-case-specific language;
- distinguish Article 6 / Annex I / Annex III classification from general operational risk.

### 3. Responsibility statements

Some existing copy implies that when a third-party AI provider is used, responsibility simply moves to the customer/deployer and that an auditor will target the customer rather than the supplier.

Risk:
- providers, deployers, importers, distributors and other actors can have different obligations;
- the allocation depends on role and facts.

Action:
- use: `Using a third-party provider does not remove your own governance responsibilities.`
- do not claim that provider responsibility disappears.

### 4. Fine-driven marketing

Existing posts lean heavily on maximum fine numbers.

Action:
- stop using fines as the primary hook;
- lead with inventory, evidence, procurement, accountability and operational readiness;
- cite official sources if a fine figure is ever material to a post.

### 5. Unsourced market statistics

At least one existing post uses adoption/governance percentages without a visible source.

Action:
- do not reuse market statistics unless the source, date and methodology are verified;
- prefer first-party product insights or official regulatory facts.

### 6. Product capability claims

Some older copy uses broad phrases such as automated documentation, continuous monitoring and audit reporting.

Action:
- reconcile every capability statement with current production functionality before reuse;
- distinguish `supports`, `helps prepare`, `tracks`, and `generates` from contractual guarantees.

## New editorial posture

Future posts should sound like an enterprise governance operator, not a regulatory fear campaign.

Preferred message hierarchy:

1. know where AI is used;
2. establish ownership;
3. classify the use case with documented rationale;
4. connect policies, vendors and evidence;
5. prepare for buyers, boards and reviewers;
6. use legal counsel for legal conclusions.
