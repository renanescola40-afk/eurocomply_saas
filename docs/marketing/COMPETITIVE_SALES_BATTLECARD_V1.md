# RISCK COMPLY — COMPETITIVE SALES BATTLECARD V1

Status: READY_FOR_SALES_EXECUTION
Checked: 2026-08-23

## 1. Purpose

Help Sales position RISCK COMPLY credibly against large AI-governance/GRC platforms and internal/manual approaches without inventing competitor pricing, capabilities or weaknesses.

Core rule:

**Do not win by attacking competitors. Win by making the buyer's operating problem explicit and showing fit.**

---

## 2. RISCK COMPLY positioning

Category:

**Operational AI Governance**

Acquisition support phrase:

**AI Governance & EU AI Act Readiness for European B2B Teams**

Core operating value:

`AI SYSTEMS -> OWNERS -> PROVIDERS -> RISK CONTEXT -> EVIDENCE -> TASKS/REVIEWS -> HISTORY`

## 3. Competitive landscape

### OneTrust

Observed official positioning/capabilities include:

- broad governance platform across AI, privacy, data use, tech risk/compliance and third-party management;
- centralized AI inventory;
- ownership/lifecycle;
- risk identification;
- configurable compliance workflows;
- evidence/audit outputs;
- continuous model/agent posture monitoring;
- telemetry and runtime guardrails/policy enforcement.

What this means:

OneTrust is a broad enterprise governance option with substantial cross-domain scope.

RISCK COMPLY should not pretend to out-feature OneTrust across the full governance stack.

Fit question:

> Do you need a broad enterprise governance/control platform spanning multiple domains and runtime enforcement, or is your immediate problem getting AI inventory, ownership, evidence and governance workflow under control for European teams?

RISCK COMPLY angle:

- focused operational AI-governance workflow;
- European readiness narrative;
- simpler category story;
- transparent evidence boundaries.

Never claim cheaper/easier/faster without verified comparison data.

Observed official positioning/capabilities include:

- graph-based AI governance;
- inventory linked to risks, policies, models and controls;
- curated knowledge graph;
- risk/control inheritance;
- workflows and integrations;
- audit/compliance readiness;

What this means:

Saidot competes directly in purpose-built AI governance and has deep regulatory/control intelligence.

RISCK COMPLY should not compete by making unsupported claims about having more frameworks or a deeper knowledge graph.

Fit question:

> Is your priority automated regulatory/control intelligence and graph-based governance across models/agents, or a straightforward operating workspace for systems, owners, risk context, evidence and cross-functional review?

RISCK COMPLY angle:

- operational clarity;
- evidence/ownership workflow;
- European B2B use;
- inventory as a commercial wedge.

## 4. Manual / spreadsheet competitor

The most common competitor may not be another SaaS vendor.

It may be:

`EXCEL + NOTION + DRIVE + EMAIL + HUMAN MEMORY`

### When manual can be sufficient

- very few systems;
- one owner;
- low change rate;
- no procurement/security evidence pressure;
- simple review process.

Do not tell every small team they need software.

### Breakpoint signals

- many teams editing the inventory;
- unclear owner;
- same provider linked to many use cases;
- reviews become stale;
- evidence is disconnected;
- customer/security questionnaires repeat;
- governance decisions need history;
- vendor/model changes trigger reassessment;
- multiple countries/jurisdictions;
- the spreadsheet becomes a workflow system by accident.

Positioning:

> The reason to move from spreadsheets is not that spreadsheets are bad. It is that the governance process has become relational, multi-owner and continuous.

## 5. Existing GRC platform competitor

Do not assume a buyer needs a second system.

Ask:

- Does the current GRC hold a live AI inventory?
- Is AI use-case/provider context modeled well?
- Can AI review rationale be preserved?
- Are evidence and review status linked to each system?
- Can governance owners work in it without excessive customization?
- Does the current process still spill into spreadsheets/Notion/email?

If current GRC solves the problem well, say so.

RISCK COMPLY only has value where a material operating gap remains.

---

## 6. Buying-decision framework

Use five fit dimensions instead of feature-count battles.

### 1. Scope

Focused AI governance vs broad enterprise governance platform.

### 2. Operational model

Static register vs continuous ownership/review/evidence workflow.

### 3. Regulatory intelligence depth

Basic readiness workflow vs extensive knowledge graph/policy/control libraries.

### 4. Technical/runtime governance

Human/business governance workflow vs telemetry/guardrails/agent/runtime enforcement.

### 5. Enterprise ecosystem

Standalone/focused adoption vs large integrated privacy/GRC/security ecosystem.

RISCK COMPLY should be chosen only where its current product truth fits the buyer's requirements.

## 7. Safe competitive language

Use:

- `may fit better if...`
- `is broader in...`
- `focuses more heavily on...`
- `RISCK COMPLY is focused on...`
- `the right choice depends on...`

Avoid:

- `competitor is overpriced`;
- `competitor is too complex` unless buyer states that experience;
- `competitor does not support X` without current evidence;
- `we are better`;
- `we have everything they have`;
- invented implementation timelines;
- invented prices;
- invented customer complaints.

## 8. Discovery routing

### Buyer says: “We are looking at OneTrust.”

Ask:

> Are you evaluating OneTrust because you want to consolidate multiple governance domains, or specifically because you need an AI-governance workflow?

If consolidation is strategic, OneTrust may be strong fit.

If the immediate pain is AI inventory/ownership/evidence, demonstrate that workflow and let buyer judge fit.

### Buyer says: “We are looking at Credo AI or Saidot.”

Ask:

> How important are regulatory knowledge-graph depth, automated control mapping, integrations and runtime/agent governance versus getting the operational inventory/evidence workflow live?

Do not imitate their strongest claims.

### Buyer says: “We already use EQS/Privacy/GRC tooling.”

Ask:

> Does your existing system already give AI teams a usable live register with owner, provider, use case, risk/evidence state and review history?

If yes, identify whether there is any real gap before selling.

## 9. RISCK COMPLY whitespace to test

Hypotheses, not proven market facts:

1. European organizations that have outgrown spreadsheets but do not want a very broad governance suite.
2. Compliance/security/legal teams that need AI-specific operating records more than runtime AI enforcement.

Validate with real pipeline before turning these into hard positioning claims.
