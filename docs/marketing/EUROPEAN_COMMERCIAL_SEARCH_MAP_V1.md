## 1. Objective

Prioritize European organic acquisition by real commercial search behavior rather than translating the same campaign into every country.

This map covers the first four acquisition fronts:

1. English-language Europe
2. Germany / DACH entry
3. France
4. Spain

Portugal remains useful as a validation and relationship market, but this map prioritizes larger organic-search acquisition opportunities.

No keyword search-volume or CPC figures are claimed in this document because no verified keyword-volume provider is connected. Priority scores are strategic relative scores based on observed SERP maturity, commercial intent, local competition, product-message fit and ability to execute with the existing RISCK COMPLY pages.

## 3. Common positioning rule

Do not lead with a generic promise such as:

> EU AI Act compliance made easy.

The category is already crowded with classifiers, document generators, consulting-led offerings and GRC suites.

Lead with:

> **Operational AI Governance**

Commercial mechanism:

`AI INVENTORY -> OWNERSHIP -> ROLE/RISK CONTEXT -> EVIDENCE -> TASKS/APPROVALS -> HISTORY -> CONTINUOUS REVIEW`

This is aligned with current RISCK COMPLY feature pages and is more defensible than claiming automatic legal compliance.

Country-local content can therefore provide unique value instead of producing doorway pages.

# 7. SPAIN

## Search-market state

Spain currently shows an important difference from Germany and France: institutional and official compliance resources occupy substantial search space.

The current Spanish ecosystem includes strong official content from:

- Ministerio para la Transformación Digital;
- AESIA;
- the Spanish AI Sandbox program;
- official practical RIA guides and checklists.

Commercial Spanish-language results are less densely populated by Spain-native governance software in the researched queries, although localized international platforms such as Sprinto appear.

This creates an opportunity for RISCK COMPLY to become an early commercial authority if content uses Spanish regulatory language accurately.

## Critical terminology lesson

Spanish official sources commonly use:

- `Reglamento de IA`
- `Reglamento europeo de Inteligencia Artificial`
- `RIA`
- `responsable del despliegue`
- `proveedor`
- `sistemas de IA`

Do not optimize everything around the English phrase `AI Act` alone.

Both language forms should be covered naturally:

> Reglamento de IA (EU AI Act)

## Spain-specific authority opportunity

AESIA/SEDIA has published a practical set of guides covering areas including:

- conformity assessment;
- quality management;
- risk management;
- human oversight;
- data governance;
- transparency;
- accuracy;
- robustness;
- cybersecurity;
- logs;
- post-market monitoring;
- incident management;
- technical documentation;
- checklists/examples.

RISCK COMPLY should not copy these materials. It should produce operational guides that reference official Spanish material and show how teams organize the work and evidence around it.

## Existing RISCK fit

RISCK already has strong Spanish pages for:

- `inventario-de-ia`
- `evaluacion-de-riesgos-de-ia`
- `gestion-de-evidencias`
- `flujos-de-gobernanza-de-ia`
- `riesgo-de-proveedores-de-ia`

The existing inventory page already targets `sistemas`, `casos de uso`, `responsables`, `proveedores`, `países`, `contexto de datos`, `evidencias` and `estado de revisión`.

## Spanish attack wedge
