## Post 1 — Article 50 is not one rule

Platform: LinkedIn
Market: Europe / English
Persona: Compliance + Legal + Security
Funnel: Awareness -> Consideration
Objective: regulatory authority

Hook:

**Article 50 is not one universal “AI label” rule. It splits duties by system type and legal role.**

Copy:

From 2 August 2026, Article 50 transparency obligations are applicable.

But the operational question is not simply: “Do we use AI?”

It is:

- Is the system interacting directly with people?
- Is it generating synthetic content?
- Are we the provider or deployer for this system?
- Is deepfake content involved?
- Is AI-generated text being published on a matter of public interest?
- Has the text undergone substantive human review or editorial control?

That is why AI governance starts with system-level facts, not a company-wide yes/no answer.

A useful governance record should preserve the system, use case, role, rationale, owner and supporting evidence behind the decision.

RISCK COMPLY is designed to keep that operating context connected.

## Post 2 — Provider vs deployer

Platform: LinkedIn
Persona: Compliance + Product + Legal
Objective: role-mapping awareness

Hook:

**Your company is not simply “a provider” or “a deployer.” The useful unit of analysis is the AI system.**

Copy:

A company can use third-party AI internally, build a separate AI feature for customers and operate another vendor system for a different workflow.

Those situations should not automatically inherit one company-wide role label.

Operationally, record:

System
Use case
Provider/vendor
Our role
Owner
Rationale
Reviewer
Evidence
Last review

That creates something far more useful than a spreadsheet column saying “EU AI Act: yes/no.”

## Post 3 — Inventory is more than tool names

Platform: LinkedIn
Persona: AI Governance + Compliance
Objective: commercial category demand

Hook:

**An AI inventory that only lists tool names is not enough to run governance.**

Copy:

“ChatGPT — Marketing”
“Copilot — Finance”
“Screening AI — HR”

That may be a useful discovery list.

But a governance inventory eventually needs enough context to answer:

- What is the business purpose?
- Who owns the use case?
- Who provides the system?
- What is our role?
- Where is it used?
- Which data context matters?
- What review was performed?
- What evidence supports the decision?
- When should it be reviewed again?

The objective is not to create the largest possible register.

It is to create a record that still makes sense when the system changes, the owner leaves, procurement asks questions or a reviewer needs the history.

That is the operating layer RISCK COMPLY is built around.

## Post 4 — Evidence is not file storage

Platform: LinkedIn
Persona: Compliance + Security + Procurement
Objective: evidence-management demand

Hook:

**A PDF in a folder is not automatically governance evidence.**

Copy:

Evidence becomes useful when a reviewer can understand:

- which AI system it relates to;
- what decision or control it supports;
- who owns it;
- when it was created or reviewed;
- whether it is still current;
- what changed afterwards.

Without that context, even a large document repository can become a search exercise whenever Legal, Security, Procurement or an auditor asks a question.

The operating model should connect evidence to systems, assessments, owners, decisions and activity history.

That is why RISCK COMPLY treats evidence as part of governance operations rather than simply file storage.

Important distinction: storing a document does not prove a control is effective.
