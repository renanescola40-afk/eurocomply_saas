# RISCK COMPLY — NURTURE, OBJECTION & PIPELINE ENGINE V1

Status: READY_FOR_MARKETING_SALES_EXECUTION / DO_NOT_MERGE_DURING_RELEASE_FREEZE
Checked: 2026-08-23

## 1. Objective

Create a repeatable bridge from content/resource/demo interest to the next commercially appropriate action.

The engine must prevent two common failures:

1. every lead immediately receives a sales push;
2. qualified buyers receive generic nurture that ignores their actual trigger.

Operating model:

`SIGNAL -> SEGMENT -> USEFUL FOLLOW-UP -> PROOF -> NEXT STEP -> RE-SCORE`

---

## 2. Segmentation

Segment first by buyer state, then by primary pain.

### Buyer state

A. EDUCATION — learning, no immediate project
B. PROBLEM AWARE — recognizes governance pain
C. SOLUTION EVALUATION — comparing workflows/vendors
D. ACTIVE PROCUREMENT — security/legal/procurement process underway
E. SELF-SERVICE — suitable for Essential/Professional evaluation

### Primary pain

- AI Inventory / Shadow AI
- EU AI Act readiness
- AI risk assessment
- Article 50 / transparency
- Provider vs Deployer
- AI governance evidence
- Vendor AI Risk / procurement
- Multi-team workflow / ownership
- Customer / board / audit request

Never send every lead the same sequence.

---

## 3. Resource lead nurture — AI Inventory Template

### Touch 0 — delivery

Subject concept:

**Your operational AI inventory template**

Purpose:

- deliver resource;
- explain how to use it;
- no hard sell.

Content:

- start with system/use case, not vendor name only;
- assign accountable owner;
- capture provider + deployment context;
- record review/evidence state;
- define next review/change trigger.

CTA:

`Use the template`

Secondary optional link:

`See how RISCK COMPLY structures AI Inventory`

### Touch 1 — 2–3 days later

Subject:

**When an AI inventory starts breaking down**

Teach the spreadsheet breakpoint:

- multiple owners;
- multiple departments;
- vendor changes;
- evidence freshness;
- review history;
- procurement requests.

CTA:

`Explore AI Inventory workflow`

### Touch 2 — 5–7 days later

Subject:

**Five questions your AI inventory should answer**

1. What AI exists?
2. Why is it used?
3. Who owns it?
4. What risk/review state applies?
5. What evidence exists and when is it reviewed again?

CTA:

`Compare your current process`

### Touch 3 — 10–14 days later

Conditional CTA by fit:

High fit/complexity:

`Book a governance demo`

Low/unknown fit:

`Read the EU AI Act operational guide`

## 5. Demo-request follow-up

### Immediately after submission

Confirm:

- request received;
- stated primary pain;
- expected call scope;
- any material they can bring.

Do not promise a response time unless operations have an accepted SLA.

Suggested preparation:

- current inventory/spreadsheet;
- vendor list;
- policy draft;
- customer questionnaire;
- board/audit request;
- security/procurement checklist.

### After demo

Send within the operating process used by Sales, without inventing a contractual response-time promise.

Follow-up should include:

1. what triggered the project;
2. what current process was observed;
3. relevant RISCK COMPLY workflow;
4. requested Trust/resource links;
5. one next action;
6. owner of that next action.

### No-decision / not-ready

Do not chase weekly forever.

Route to a useful nurture based on stated pain and timing.

Re-enter direct sales when:

- buyer replies;
- new procurement trigger appears;
- demo requested again;
- significant product activity indicates intent once measurement is available;
- agreed review date arrives.

## 6. Active procurement sequence

Use only when a real procurement/security review exists.

Sequence:

1. Trust Center
2. Security overview
3. DPA/subprocessor material as currently approved
4. Questionnaire / requested evidence
5. Open gaps clearly identified
6. Contract/legal handoff where applicable
7. Product/governance review only where buyer needs it

Do not flood a security reviewer with marketing content.

Do not send draft legal material as final approved contractual truth.

---

## 7. Objection-to-content routing

### “We use spreadsheets”

Send:

- AI Inventory Template
- spreadsheet breakpoint article
- AI Inventory feature

### “We already have GRC”

Send:

- AI governance operating-model comparison
- integration/fit discussion if product truth supports it
- no blanket replacement pitch

### “We are not sure what applies under the AI Act”

Send:

- August 2026 flagship guide
- Provider vs Deployer
- Article 50 guide if relevant

Then separate legal interpretation from operational workflow.

### “Security/procurement will block this”

Send:

- Trust Center
- security overview
- subprocessors/current DPA posture
- Enterprise review

### “We are too early”

Send:

- inventory template
- maturity assessment when available
- regulatory updates

Do not push a demo every time.

### “We have hundreds of AI systems”

Route:

- Business/Enterprise conversation;
- inventory/ownership model;
- rollout/procurement discussion;
- capacity/contract truth from canonical pricing/billing.

## 8. Lost-opportunity taxonomy

Every meaningful lost/stalled opportunity should receive one primary reason:

- no priority / no trigger;
- no budget;
- internal build;
- spreadsheet sufficient;
- existing GRC sufficient;
- missing feature;
- security requirement;
- legal/procurement blocker;
- product maturity concern;
- company maturity/trust concern;
- timing delayed;
- competitor selected;
- no response;
- not ICP;
- other documented reason.

Do not classify `no response` as `price` or another guessed reason.

Use lost reasons to drive product/marketing work only after enough real evidence accumulates.

---

## 9. Pipeline stages

Recommended commercial stages:

1. New lead
2. Qualified / nurture
3. Demo scheduled
4. Demo completed
5. Solution fit confirmed
6. Security/procurement review
7. Commercial proposal / checkout route
8. Contract / payment pending
9. Customer
10. Closed lost / deferred

Self-service can skip sales stages.

Do not treat a content download as an opportunity.

---

## 10. Handoff contract between Marketing and Sales

Marketing passes:

- source/campaign;
- landing/content;
- market/locale;
- primary pain;
- company-size band;
- AI-system-count band when known;
- timeline;
- lead priority band;
- resources consumed where privacy-safe.

Sales returns:

- qualified/not qualified;
- pain confirmed/corrected;
- buying group;
- procurement/security need;
- next step;
- lost reason;
- opportunity stage;
- commercial outcome.

This feedback loop is required to improve content priorities.

---

## 11. Founder-content assist capture

When a buyer mentions a founder/company post, guide or resource during a demo, record an assist value rather than replacing the original acquisition source.

Examples:

- `content_assist=founder_inventory_post`
- `content_assist=august_2026_guide`
- `content_assist=article50_carousel`
- `content_assist=inventory_template`

One deal may have multiple assists.

Do not force single-touch attribution onto a complex B2B buying journey.

---

## 12. Nurture metrics

Measure:

- resource-to-feature visit;
- resource-to-demo;
- guide-to-returning-visitor;
- nurture-to-demo;
- nurture-to-self-service;
- Priority A/B reactivation;
- days from first touch to demo;
- content assists per opportunity;
- unsubscribe rate when email tooling exists;
- lost reason distribution;
- market/locale differences.

Do not optimize email open rate as the primary KPI.

---

## 13. Privacy and consent

Email marketing/nurture must use an appropriate lawful/consent basis and actual configured communication preferences.

The current `consentToContact` on a demo form authorizes sales/demo follow-up language; do not silently reinterpret it as permission for unlimited marketing newsletters without verifying the legal/product consent model.

Keep personal identity and free-text lead details out of product analytics.
