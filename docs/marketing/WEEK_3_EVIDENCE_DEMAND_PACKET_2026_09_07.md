Campaign: `FOUNDER-003`
Theme: evidence != files
Audience: Compliance, Security/GRC, Legal, Procurement, AI Governance
CTA: conversation

## Publish copy — English

Most AI governance teams do not have an evidence shortage.

They have an evidence-context problem.

There are PDFs in Drive.
There are screenshots in tickets.
There are vendor documents in procurement.
There are decisions in email.
There are owners in someone’s head.

The problem appears six months later when someone asks:

- what AI system did this evidence relate to?
- what decision did it support?
- who relied on it?
- was it still valid at the time?
- what changed afterward?

A useful evidence record connects:

system/use case -> decision -> owner -> implementation -> evidence -> review -> history.

A file becomes governance evidence when another reviewer can understand what it proves without reconstructing the whole story from scratch.

Question for AI governance teams: if I picked one material AI use case today, could you reconstruct that chain in 10 minutes?

# TUESDAY 8 SEP — COMPANY POST

Campaign: `EN-EVIDENCE-003`
Theme: evidence quality
CTA: evidence framework / conversation

## Publish copy — English

**A document is not automatically evidence.**

For AI governance, useful evidence should pass six basic tests:

1. Relevance — does it support the decision being reviewed?
2. Specificity — is it tied to the correct system/use case?
3. Authority — is the source strong enough for the claim?
4. Freshness — is it still current?
5. Traceability — can you see who relied on it and why?
6. Reproducibility — could another reviewer reconstruct the decision path?

This is why evidence management should not begin with “upload a PDF”.

It should begin with the governance record the PDF belongs to.

RISCK COMPLY is designed to help teams connect AI systems/use cases with owners, governance context, evidence, actions and review history.

It does not turn an uploaded document into a certification or legal conclusion.

# WEDNESDAY 9 SEP — FOUNDER POST

Campaign: `FOUNDER-003`
Theme: stale evidence
CTA: conversation

## Publish copy — English

The most dangerous governance evidence is often not missing evidence.

It is **stale evidence that still looks valid**.

A vendor security document may have been current at onboarding.
A role assessment may have made sense before the product changed.
A disclosure may have been reviewed before the UX changed.
A model-provider statement may pre-date a migration.

If the evidence has no review trigger, the organisation is relying on memory to know when it stopped being trustworthy.

A better pattern is:

Evidence -> valid context -> owner -> next review -> material-change trigger.

Typical triggers:

- model/provider change;
- new use case;
- new deployment region;
- material feature change;
- security/privacy change;
- customer/procurement requirement;
- regulatory/guidance change.

Governance should not ask only “do we have evidence?”

It should also ask “what would make this evidence stale?”

# FRIDAY 11 SEP — BUYER PROBLEM POST

Campaign: `FOUNDER-003`
Theme: customer/procurement review
CTA: evidence operating model

## Publish copy — English

Customer AI questionnaires are expensive when every answer starts from zero.

The same questions come back in different forms:

- what AI systems are in scope?
- who owns them?
- what provider/model is used?
- what review happened?
- what evidence supports the answer?
- what changed since the last review?

If Security, Product, Legal and Procurement have to rebuild the context every time, the questionnaire is not the root problem.

The root problem is that governance evidence is not connected to the system/use-case record.

A reusable evidence model should let the team answer once, review when facts change, and reuse only what is still valid.

That is a much better operating model than copying last quarter’s questionnaire and hoping nothing changed.
