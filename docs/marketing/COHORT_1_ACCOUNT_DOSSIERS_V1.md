## 1. SEEDEXT — FRANCE

Account type: PROSPECT
Priority: A
Primary wedge: enterprise AI evidence + use-case governance

### Current public signals

Seedext currently positions itself as a French secure AI note-taking product for businesses, with enterprise-oriented pricing, integrations with Teams, Zoom, Outlook and CRM, French-hosted encrypted data, isolated customer environments and a sovereign/responsible-European-AI message.

Current product motion also emphasises structured meeting outputs, long-term knowledge, team/client sharing and enterprise-scale deployment.

### Operating hypothesis

As enterprise deployments grow across users, meeting contexts and customer environments, the governance challenge may shift from product security alone to preserving:

- system/use-case context;
- owner;
- deployment/customer context;
- transparency/review requirements where relevant;
- supporting evidence;
- review history.

This is a hypothesis to validate, not a statement about Seedext's current internal process.

### Best conversation angle

`secure enterprise AI -> customer/procurement evidence -> use-case inventory -> ongoing review`

### Buyer functions

1. Product / CTO
2. Security / Privacy
3. Enterprise Sales / Customer Trust
4. Legal / Compliance

### Useful asset

Article 50 + Evidence Operating Map

### Avoid

- telling Seedext that Article 50 automatically requires a specific disclosure in every deployment;
- questioning its existing security claims without evidence;
- pitching generic AI Act compliance.

### Discovery question

When enterprise customers ask how a particular AI meeting use case is governed, is the answer already stored as one traceable system/use-case record, or assembled from product, security, privacy and customer-success evidence separately?

## 2. JIMINI AI — FRANCE

Account type: PROSPECT
Priority: A+
Primary wedge: legal AI trust + governance evidence

### Current public signals

Jimini publicly positions itself as sovereign French legal AI for continental-law professionals and reports use by more than 6,000 lawyers and legal professionals.

Its 2026 public activity includes a deployment by the University of Mons, partnerships with legal-sector organisations and Tomorro, and a strong sovereignty message.

Jimini also operates a public Trust Center showing ISO 27001 documentation and a substantial control set.

### Operating hypothesis

Jimini already demonstrates strong trust/security maturity. The more credible wedge is therefore not `you need a Trust Center`.

The potential operating problem is connecting customer-facing trust evidence with the AI-governance record behind specific legal AI systems/use cases:

- system/use-case ownership;
- model/provider dependencies;
- review rationale;
- evidence history;
- customer assurance context;
- change/review triggers.

### Best conversation angle

`existing strong trust posture -> AI-specific operating evidence -> system/use-case traceability`

### Buyer functions

1. Product / AI
2. Security / Trust
3. Legal / Governance
4. Enterprise / Partnerships

### Useful asset

AI Governance Evidence Model

### Avoid

- implying Jimini lacks compliance/security controls;
- leading with generic procurement pain when they already have a mature Trust Center;
- claiming RISCK COMPLY can improve legal classification accuracy.

### Discovery question

You already expose a mature trust posture publicly. For the AI-specific layer, do you keep the rationale, owner, evidence and review history for each material system/use case in the same operating model, or does that live across separate product/security/legal processes?

## 3. GENOW — GERMANY

Account type: PROSPECT
Priority: A
Primary wedge: enterprise procurement evidence + AI inventory

### Current public signals

Genow currently positions itself as an AI platform for industrial sales, deployed in the customer's cloud, with Microsoft/Google SSO, customer permissions and data remaining in the customer's environment.

Its public site states ISO 27001 certification, GDPR compliance and EU AI Act readiness. A 2026 Hannover Messe campaign also targeted new B2B customers with managed GCP PoC support.

### Operating hypothesis

Because Genow sits inside enterprise workflows and customer environments, customer assurance may require more than product-level security statements as deployments multiply.

Potential governance questions include:

- which customer deployment/use case exists;
- owner and business context;
- what AI/model/provider dependencies apply;
- what evidence supports enterprise assurance;
- when the record needs re-review.

### Best conversation angle

`customer-cloud enterprise deployments -> deployment inventory -> procurement evidence`

### Buyer functions

1. Founder / CTO
2. Security / Platform
3. Enterprise Sales
4. Product / Delivery

### Useful asset

AI System Inventory Template — Enterprise Deployment Edition

### Avoid

- challenging Genow's public ISO certification;
- saying EU AI Act-ready is equivalent to legal compliance;
- pitching a replacement for its product-level governance/security controls.

### Discovery question

As customer-cloud deployments accumulate, how do you keep the deployment/use-case record, owner, AI dependencies and supporting assurance evidence consistent across enterprise accounts?

## 4. SUBSTRATE AI — SPAIN

Account type: STRATEGIC PROSPECT
Priority: A+
Primary wedge: AI portfolio inventory + provider/customer evidence

### Current public signals

On 30 June 2026, Spain's SETT announced a €19.1m public investment in Substrate AI as part of a €39m capital increase.

The official announcement states that Substrate AI has more than 500 customers, more than 200 employees and AI products spanning agent-creation/management software and cloud-compute infrastructure, across sectors including health, industry and technology.

### Operating hypothesis

A diversified AI portfolio across products, sectors and customers can create governance complexity at portfolio level:

- system/product inventory;
- provider/deployer context per use case;
- accountable owners;
- model/infrastructure dependencies;
- evidence by product/customer;
- review/change history.

This does not imply a deficiency in Substrate AI's current governance.

### Best conversation angle

`multi-product scale -> AI portfolio operating model -> customer/provider evidence`

### Buyer functions

1. CTO / AI Platform
2. Legal / Compliance
3. Security
4. Enterprise Operations / Customer Trust

### Useful asset

AI Portfolio Governance Map

### Avoid

- using public investment as a fake urgency trigger;
- implying government investment means endorsement of RISCK COMPLY;
- making sector-specific legal-risk assertions without facts.

### Discovery question

With multiple AI products and customer contexts, is governance managed primarily product by product, or do you already maintain one portfolio-level view connecting systems, owners, role context, evidence and review status?

## 5. TIMBAL AI — SPAIN

Account type: PROSPECT / POSSIBLE COMPLEMENTARY GOVERNANCE FIT
Priority: A
Primary wedge: agent/use-case inventory + cross-functional governance history

### Current public signals

Timbal positions itself as a European production AI platform for enterprise teams to build, deploy and govern agents, workflows and knowledge bases.

Current public product material includes managed deployments, traceability/observability, environments, Git integration, EU-region hosting, model/provider choice, Trust Center/DPA/subprocessor surfaces and an Action Control Engine for deterministic policy enforcement.

Its recent public content emphasises moving enterprise AI from demos to production with reliability, control and scalability.

### Operating hypothesis

Timbal already has technical runtime governance, observability and policy controls. RISCK COMPLY should therefore not pitch `governance` generically.

The potential complementary layer is cross-functional operating governance outside runtime execution:

- enterprise inventory across agents/use cases;
- business owners;
- legal/compliance context;
- evidence packs;
- procurement/customer-review history;
- review obligations beyond runtime traces.

### Best conversation angle

`runtime governance already strong -> cross-functional governance record -> procurement/evidence layer`

### Buyer functions

1. Founder / CTO
2. Product / Platform
3. Security / Trust
4. Enterprise Sales

### Useful asset

Technical Governance vs Operational Governance Boundary Map

### Avoid

- presenting RISCK COMPLY as a replacement for Timbal observability/policy enforcement;
- claiming they lack governance;
- leading with basic AI inventory as if their platform does not already govern technical assets.

### Discovery question

You already cover runtime governance and observability. On the cross-functional side, how do customers connect those technical records to business ownership, legal/compliance rationale, procurement evidence and recurring governance review?
