**Status:** `REVIEW_DRAFT` · `FOUNDER_FACT_REQUIRED` · `COUNSEL_DECISION_REQUIRED`  
**Important:** This is a negotiation draft, not an executed agreement or legal opinion. It must be adapted and approved by qualified counsel before signature.

## Parties

1. **Platform party:** `[LEGAL ENTITY NAME]`, registration `[NUMBER]`, VAT `[NUMBER]`, registered address `[ADDRESS]`, operating the Risck Comply software platform (`Platform`).
2. **Counsel party:** `[LAWYER OR LAW FIRM LEGAL NAME]`, professional registration `[REGISTRATION]`, jurisdiction `[JURISDICTION]`, address `[ADDRESS]` (`Counsel`).

Together, the `Parties`.

## 1. Purpose

The Parties intend to collaborate on the professional review and practical use of Risck Comply as a software tool for AI governance, EU AI Act readiness, evidence preparation and workflow management.

The collaboration may include:

- review of product methodology and legal-facing materials;
- review of defined legal workstreams;
- controlled pilot use by Counsel;
- product feedback and remediation;
- optional training or educational content;
- a professional software licence for Counsel;
- separate legal engagements between Counsel and customers.

This Agreement does not itself appoint Counsel for any customer matter, create a law firm, create a multidisciplinary company, authorise the Platform to practise law or guarantee any review outcome.

## 2. Independent services

The Platform supplies software and technical preparation services. Counsel supplies legal services only under a separate engagement accepted by Counsel.

Nothing in this Agreement:

- makes Counsel an employee, agent or representative of the Platform;
- makes the Platform a law firm or legal-services provider;
- permits the Platform to direct a legal conclusion;
- obliges Counsel to approve the product or any customer result;
- creates a lawyer-client relationship between Counsel and a Platform user without a separate engagement;
- creates exclusivity unless expressly added in a signed schedule.

Counsel retains sole control over professional judgment, conflicts, client acceptance, legal scope, advice, findings and signature.

## 3. Initial no-cash or reduced-cash collaboration

Because the Platform currently has limited financial resources, the Parties may select one of the following structures in Schedule 1:

### Option A — Defined licence-for-review exchange

The Platform provides a professional licence for a fixed period. Counsel provides a defined number of review hours and specified deliverables.

### Option B — Limited pro bono review

Counsel voluntarily performs a strictly defined review without a fee. No future free work is implied.

### Option C — Fixed deferred fee

The Parties agree a fixed amount, due on a defined future date or event. The amount is not contingent on a favourable legal conclusion.

### Option D — Pilot licence

Counsel receives controlled access to use the Platform in agreed pilot matters and provides structured product findings. Customer legal services remain separately contracted.

### Option E — Discounted professional subscription

Counsel pays a reduced software subscription and receives no obligation to provide legal work beyond separately agreed scopes.

No option applies until completed and signed in Schedule 1.

## 4. No improper fee sharing or referral control

Unless a lawful structure is independently confirmed for every relevant jurisdiction:

- the Platform will not receive a percentage of Counsel's legal fees;
- Counsel will not pay the Platform a commission merely for introducing a legal client;
- software fees will not be disguised as a share of legal fees;
- Counsel's payment will not depend on a favourable opinion;
- the Platform will not control billing, trust money, client funds or professional retainers;
- customer software contracts and legal engagements will remain separate;
- each Party will issue its own invoices for its own services.

If the Parties later consider referral fees, multidisciplinary practice, revenue sharing, bundled billing or a marketplace commission, they must first obtain written professional-regulation advice and amend this Agreement.

## 5. Review scope

Counsel's initial review scope is limited to the items selected in Schedule 2. Potential workstreams include:

1. legal rules registry;
2. prohibited practices;
3. Article 50 transparency;
4. FRIA methodology;
5. deployer obligations;
6. high-risk provider controls;
7. conformity, declaration, CE and registration workflow;
8. GPAI obligations;
9. intended purpose and product classification;
10. claims, Terms, Privacy, DPA and partner model.

Excluded scope must be listed expressly. Silence does not expand scope.

## 6. Review materials and exact-SHA binding

The Platform will provide a counsel handoff package containing:

- source-code SHA;
- package digest;
- product dossier;
- intended purpose;
- source register;
- article-to-evidence matrix;
- relevant implementation and test evidence;
- runtime evidence;
- known limitations;
- founder facts supplied and unresolved;
- decision templates.

Counsel's decision should identify the reviewed SHA and evidence digest. A later product version is not automatically covered.

## 7. Counsel deliverables

Subject to the selected schedule, Counsel may provide:

- global decision sheet;
- findings and required changes;
- redlines;
- one decision per reviewed workstream;
- permitted reliance and exclusions;
- validity period;
- change triggers;
- signed opinion or decision reference;
- confirmation that identified remediation has been reviewed.

Counsel may return `ACCEPTED`, `ACCEPTED_WITH_CHANGES`, `CHANGES_REQUIRED`, `REJECTED` or `OUTSIDE_SCOPE`.

## 8. Platform obligations

The Platform will:

- provide accurate technical materials to the best of its knowledge;
- identify unresolved founder facts;
- disclose known limitations;
- not conceal material product behavior;
- preserve evidence integrity and change history;
- remediate agreed findings where technically controlled;
- not alter Counsel's conclusions;
- not publish Counsel's name, logo, quotation or approval without written permission;
- not describe a limited review as certification of the entire product;
- not claim guaranteed customer compliance.

## 9. Counsel obligations

Counsel will:

- confirm professional registration and jurisdiction;
- perform conflict and independence checks;
- define scope and exclusions;
- protect confidential information;
- review primary sources where professionally required;
- exercise independent judgment;
- identify when specialist or jurisdiction-specific advice is required;
- avoid approving matters outside competence;
- notify the Platform if professional standing materially changes;
- comply with applicable professional and ethical rules.

## 10. Customer matters

For each customer legal matter:

- Counsel decides whether to accept the engagement;
- Counsel performs required identification, conflicts and engagement steps;
- the customer receives Counsel's identity, scope, exclusions, fees and complaint route;
- Counsel controls professional advice and correspondence;
- the Platform provides software under its own agreement;
- the customer remains responsible for accurate business and system facts;
- a Platform score is not a legal opinion;
- formal conformity or regulator procedures remain separate where applicable.

## 11. Confidentiality

Each Party will protect the other Party's confidential information and use it only for the collaboration.

Confidential information includes source code, security details, customer data, pricing, unpublished product plans, legal analysis, work product and review findings.

Exclusions should cover information that is public without breach, already lawfully known, independently developed or lawfully received from a third party.

Required disclosure must, where lawful, be preceded by notice and limited to the required scope.

## 12. Legal privilege and professional secrecy

The Parties acknowledge that privilege and professional secrecy depend on jurisdiction, purpose, participants and handling.

- use of the Platform does not automatically create privilege;
- confidential legal material must use restricted workspaces;
- public repositories must not contain signed opinions, identification documents or unredacted confidential advice;
- the Platform may store only safe references and digests in public evidence;
- Counsel decides whether a customer communication is privileged;
- access by providers and Platform personnel must follow the DPA, least privilege and documented operational need.

No Party guarantees privilege.

## 13. Data protection

Before processing personal data, the Parties will determine their roles for each activity.

Potential positions include:

- Platform as processor for customer workspace data;
- Counsel as independent controller for legal engagements;
- Counsel as processor only where the actual engagement supports that role;
- separate controllers for partnership administration.

The Parties will document:

- purposes and lawful bases;
- data categories;
- data subjects;
- access;
- subprocessors;
- transfers;
- retention;
- security measures;
- incident contacts;
- deletion or return;
- data-subject request cooperation.

No special-category or highly sensitive personal data should be uploaded unless necessary, permitted and protected by an agreed workflow.

## 14. Security

The Platform will apply the security controls represented in its current evidence package, subject to stated limitations. Counsel will:

- use unique accounts;
- enable required MFA;
- not share credentials;
- restrict exports;
- notify suspected compromise;
- avoid downloading unnecessary customer data;
- follow secure-device and secure-communication practices.

Security incidents affecting shared data will be handled under the applicable DPA and incident schedule.

## 15. Intellectual property

The Platform retains ownership of the software, code, platform documentation, generic templates and pre-existing materials.

Counsel retains ownership of pre-existing legal know-how, professional work product and materials that applicable law or professional rules require Counsel to control.

Unless Schedule 3 states otherwise:

- feedback may be used by the Platform without identifying confidential client information;
- customer-specific legal advice is not transferred to the Platform;
- generic product corrections derived from findings may be implemented by the Platform;
- neither Party may use the other's trademarks without written approval;
- no licence extends beyond the stated purpose.

## 16. Publicity and co-marketing

No public statement may imply:

- EU or regulator approval;
- product certification;
- guaranteed compliance;
- Counsel approval beyond signed scope;
- Counsel employment by the Platform;
- an exclusive partnership;
- a lawyer-client relationship for every Platform user.

Any use of Counsel's name, biography, logo, quotation, webinar appearance or review statement requires prior written approval of the exact wording and duration.

## 17. Liability

The Parties' liabilities must remain separate.

- the Platform is responsible for its software commitments under its contracts;
- Counsel is responsible for legal services under separate engagements;
- the Platform does not provide professional indemnity cover for Counsel;
- Counsel does not guarantee software uptime or security;
- neither Party may expand the other's obligations without authority.

Liability caps, excluded losses, indemnities and insurance requirements remain `[COUNSEL DECISION REQUIRED]` and should be aligned with the final commercial model and mandatory law.

## 18. Complaints

Software complaints go to the Platform under its customer terms. Legal-service complaints go to Counsel under the applicable engagement and professional regime.

Customer-facing materials must not obscure the correct route.

## 19. Term and termination

This Agreement begins on `[DATE]` and continues for `[TERM]` unless terminated under this section.

Either Party may terminate on `[NOTICE PERIOD]` written notice. Immediate termination may apply for:

- material breach not cured within an agreed period;
- professional suspension or disqualification;
- confidentiality or security breach;
- misuse of brand;
- misleading compliance claims;
- improper interference with professional independence;
- unlawful fee arrangement.

Termination does not erase confidentiality, data-protection, intellectual-property, payment, liability or evidence-preservation obligations that should survive.

## 20. Exit handling

On termination:

- access is revoked according to the agreed schedule;
- customer matters remain governed by their separate engagements;
- customer choice and professional duties are preserved;
- confidential data is returned or deleted subject to retention duties;
- public partnership references are removed within the agreed period;
- signed decisions remain historical evidence but cannot be represented as current beyond validity or changed scope.

## 21. Governing law and disputes

Governing law, courts, mediation and professional-regulator jurisdiction are `[FOUNDER FACT AND COUNSEL DECISION REQUIRED]`.

Professional obligations applicable to Counsel remain unaffected by a contractual choice of law.

## 22. Entire agreement and amendments

This Agreement and signed schedules form the collaboration agreement. Amendments must be in writing and signed by authorised representatives.

No email, dashboard status, AI output or unsigned template modifies Counsel's professional decision.
