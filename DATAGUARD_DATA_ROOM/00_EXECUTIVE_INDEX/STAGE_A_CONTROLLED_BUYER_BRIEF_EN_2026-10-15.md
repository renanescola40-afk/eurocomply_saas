## 1. Opportunity summary

RISCK COMPLY is an enterprise-oriented software platform supporting AI-system inventory, governance, regulatory risk classification, documentation, audit/evidence workflows, and EU AI Act readiness operations.

The potential transaction is a **strategic acquisition of agreed software/IP assets**, rather than a revenue-multiple case. No amount, offer, bidder commitment or accepted valuation is asserted.

## 2. Technology and intended use

The documented software architecture uses Next.js/TypeScript, Supabase Auth/Postgres with tenant-oriented RLS and application authorization, and provider integrations including Stripe for commercial billing. The repository includes governance workflow implementations, documentation, security controls and release/testing infrastructure.

Examples of supported product workflows described in the source material:

- Maintain an organization-owned AI systems register.
- Assign accountable stakeholders and document risk/classification context.
- Maintain governance and review evidence with supporting documentation.
- Support internal audit, monitoring, procurement and accountability workflows.

This summary describes **documented/source-implemented scope**. It is not a claim that every module has been freshly proven in the currently deployed production revision.

## 3. AI Act and assurance boundaries

RISCK COMPLY helps customers organize AI-governance work; the platform **does not grant regulatory compliance, legal advice, certification or formal audit acceptance**.

The source materials describe application security, role controls, RLS tenant boundaries, CI/security review and independent assessment history. **No ISO 27001 certification, SOC 2 report, clean terminal independent pentest, independently assured tenant isolation or zero-vulnerability guarantee is claimed.**

A historic external web-app assessment is documented; applicable final retest and release-specific assurances remain diligence items.

## 4. Business and transaction facts

RISCK COMPLY is positioned as a **pre-commercial technology asset**, without a claimed established customer base or recurring revenue history. Available point-in-time Stripe evidence is scoped to that provider and is not conclusive about total entity financial statements, off-platform contracts or bank movements.

Potential transferable perimeter, subject to proof and agreement: seller-owned software code, documentation, database/schema assets, design and brand rights, domain and operational know-how. Repository administration does **not** independently prove seller ownership of copyright or transferable title.

Creator-to-seller IP chain, official seller authority, provider-specific migration rights, historical/source licenses, complete accounting and transaction-specific contracts require separate primary evidence.

## 5. Strategic integration paths to evaluate

A buyer may assess use as a standalone product, as an AI-governance capability inside a broader risk/compliance portfolio, or as software integrated with an enterprise trust and evidence platform. Integration time, rebuild avoidance and commercial value require buyer-led assessment and are **not assigned an invented number** here.

## 6. Due diligence / demo protocol

An introductory product demo can illustrate product workflows, but it should not be presented as signed buyer acceptance, source-to-production parity or proof of operational security.

Subsequent controlled diligence may include detailed technical architecture, exact-SHA source/CI evidence, provider status, OSS/license registers and chain-of-title/financial materials after approved NDA, least-privilege access and qualified review.

**Not included in this briefing:** source-code access, provider account access, security exploit details, personal data, corporate inbox correspondence, raw scans, confidential client/buyer names, privileged legal advice, credentials or any authorization to migrate production services.
